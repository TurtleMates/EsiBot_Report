\section{Differential Architecture}
\label{sec:meca_diff}

\esibot{} adopts a \emph{differential drive} architecture: two independent driven wheels
plus a caster wheel ensuring static stability. The chassis \gls{cad}/STL files form a
separate deliverable (Deliverable~1); this chapter relies on the \gls{urdf} model
(\texttt{esibot\_description}) and the electronic constants found in the control code.

\section{Differential Kinematics and Odometry}
\label{sec:meca_kinematics}

The \topic{/cmd\_vel} $\to$ wheel speed conversion, implemented in
\texttt{motor\_bridge\_node.py} (\texttt{twist\_to\_wheel\_\allowbreak commands}) and
replicated in the dashboard (\texttt{imu\_web\_dashboard.py}), follows the classic
model:

\begin{equation}
	v_{\text{l}} = v - \omega \cdot \tfrac{L}{2}, \quad
	v_{\text{r}} = v + \omega \cdot \tfrac{L}{2}
	\label{eq:diff_drive}
\end{equation}

and conversely for odometry reconstruction:
$v = (v_{\text{l}}+v_{\text{r}})/2$, $\omega = (v_{\text{r}}-v_{\text{l}})/L$
(equation~\ref{eq:diff_drive_inverse}), where $L$ is the wheelbase.
\begin{equation}
	v = \frac{v_{\text{l}} + v_{\text{r}}}{2}, \qquad
	\omega = \frac{v_{\text{r}} - v_{\text{l}}}{L}
	\label{eq:diff_drive_inverse}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{images/diff_drive_diagram.png}
	\caption{Kinematic model of the \esibot{} differential robot (top view).}
	\label{fig:diff_drive_diagram}
\end{figure}

Each wheel is fitted with an encoder counted via \gls{gpio} interrupt, with 2\,ms
software debouncing. The \texttt{encoder\_cpr} parameter (12 by default) is doubled by
counting on both edges (\texttt{encoder\_count\_both\_\allowbreak edges=True}), giving
\textbf{24 pulses/revolution}. The measured rotation speed is computed at each control
cycle (\texttt{control\_hz}, 10\,Hz by default) using:
\begin{equation}
	\text{RPM}_{\text{measured}} = \frac{N_{\text{pulses}}}{\text{CPR}_{\text{effective}}} \times \frac{60}{\Delta t}
	\label{eq:rpm_measure}
\end{equation}
i.e. $360^\circ/24=15^\circ$ per pulse, and, with the calibrator's 0.033\,m radius,
$\approx$\textbf{8.6\,mm}/pulse ($2\pi\times0.033/24$).

\section{Electronic Wiring}
\label{ch:electronique}
\label{sec:elec_topology}

The specification (Deliverable~2) requires a Fritzing/KiCad schematic, which is absent
from the provided archive; Table~\ref{tab:gpio_pinout} was therefore \textbf{reconstructed
by reverse engineering} from the \gls{gpio} constants in the Python code. Unlike the
specification, which planned for the ESP32-CAM to drive the H-bridge, the servo and the
HC-SR04, the actual implementation concentrates all real-time electronics on the
\gls{gpio} pins of the Raspberry\,Pi\,4.

In the \texttt{working\_build} branch, the calibrator's software wiring and that of the
production node are consistent: both use BCM pins~26 and~27 for the left and right
encoders. The PWM$\to$RPM calibration can therefore directly feed
\texttt{pwm\_mapper.py}, provided the wheel radius and wheelbase used by the odometry are
experimentally verified (see Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations}).

Motor driving uses a dual \textbf{L298N} H-bridge with software PWM (\texttt{RPi.GPIO},
500\,Hz), with duty cycle bounded to $[15\,\%,100\,\%]$
(\texttt{min/max\_duty\_cycle}) to compensate for mechanical dead zone. The MPU6050
(\gls{i2c} bus~1, address \texttt{0x68}) is configured at startup (wake-up, sample-rate
divider \texttt{SMPLRT\_DIV=0x07}, low-pass filter \texttt{DLPF\_CFG=0x03},
$\pm2g$/$\pm250^\circ$/s) and publishes at \textbf{50\,Hz}. The Kinect (v1, Xbox\,360
era), the primary mapping sensor, is connected via \textbf{USB} (not \gls{gpio}) and
driven by the community package \texttt{kinect\_ros2} (\texttt{libfreenect}).

\section{Camera Server (ESP32-CAM)}
\label{sec:elec_esp32cam}

The ESP32-CAM module (\texttt{AI\_THINKER}) is used exclusively as a \gls{wifi}
\gls{mjpeg} video server: the \texttt{CameraServerArduinoIDE.ino} firmware initializes
the OV2640 sensor (\texttt{UXGA} if PSRAM is present, otherwise \texttt{SVGA}) and
exposes the stream on \texttt{/stream}. No \gls{gpio} pin of the ESP32-CAM drives an
actuator --- most of its pins being reserved for the parallel camera interface --- a
hardware constraint likely behind the move of motor control to the Raspberry\,Pi\,4. The
\gls{wifi} credentials (SSID/password) are \textbf{hardcoded} in the firmware, with no
captive portal or provisioning (Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations}).

\section{Power Supply}
\label{sec:elec_power}

The architecture diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:global_archi}) identifies a "Power
Management" block separately supplying the actuator layer (L298N) and the compute layer
(Raspberry\,Pi\,4, ESP32-CAM). No battery supervision node
(\topic{/battery\_state}, planned in the specification) was found in the code: battery
telemetry is, at this stage, not exposed by the onboard software.
