\chapter{User Interface and Implementation}
\label{ch:ihm}

\section{Xbox Controller Teleoperation}
\label{sec:ihm_teleop}

Teleoperation relies on a wireless (Xbox-compatible) controller, driven by
\texttt{joy\_node}/\texttt{teleop\_twist\_joy} (\texttt{joystick\_drive.launch.py}),
rather than the \texttt{teleop\_twist\_keyboard} suggested in the specification.

\begin{table}[H]
	\centering
	\caption{Controller teleoperation configuration (\texttt{xbox\_teleop.yaml}).}
	\label{tab:teleop_params}
	\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{L{5.5cm} C{2.6cm} L{4.3cm}}
		\toprule
		\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Note} \\
		\midrule
		\texttt{require\_enable\_button} & \texttt{false} & no "dead-man" button required \\
		Linear / angular axis & axis 1 (scale 0.25) / axis 3 (scale 2.4) & sticks $\to$ \topic{/cmd\_vel} \\
		Dead zone / repeat rate & 0.08 / 20\,Hz & \texttt{joy\_node} \\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The absence of a mandatory enable button means that any accidental stick deflection
immediately translates into motion, with no button to hold down --- a reduced safety
margin compared to a classic "dead-man" device (Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations}).
\texttt{joystick\_drive.launch.py} jointly starts \texttt{joy\_node},
\texttt{teleop\_twist\_joy\_node} and \texttt{motor\_bridge\_node} (max angular velocity
raised to 3.0\,rad/s, minimum duty cycle 22\,\%).

\section{\gls{ekf} Fusion --- Configuration Detail}
\label{sec:ihm_ekf_detail}

\texttt{ekf\_odom\_imu.yaml} (\texttt{ekf\_fusion.launch.py}) configures
\texttt{ekf\_filter\_node} at \textbf{30\,Hz}, \texttt{two\_d\_mode}: from odometry
(\topic{/odom}), only the \emph{velocities} $(v_x,v_y,\dot\theta)$ are fused; from the
\gls{imu} (\topic{/imu/data\_raw}), only $\dot\theta$ is kept. This configuration avoids
integrating the MPU6050's raw acceleration or an orientation that the node does not
provide. The \texttt{world\_frame} remains \texttt{odom}, leaving the
\topic{map}$\to$\topic{odom} correction to \texttt{slam\_toolbox}/\gls{amcl} --- a clean
separation between local (\gls{ekf}) and global (\gls{slam}/\gls{amcl}) estimation.

\section{Telemetry Web Dashboard}
\label{sec:ihm_dashboard}

The \texttt{imu\_web\_dashboard} node (\texttt{esibot\_dashboard}) exposes a standalone
HTTP server (\texttt{http.server}, no external dependency), serving a single-page
HTML/JS page, noticeably different from the dashboard envisioned in the specification
(which called for a \texttt{rosbridge\_suite} WebSocket gateway with a map, video and
teleoperation buttons) --- the actual implementation focuses on \textbf{telemetry}.

\begin{table}[H]
	\centering
	\caption{Widgets of the \texttt{imu\_web\_dashboard}.}
	\label{tab:dashboard_widgets}
	\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{L{3.2cm} L{8.7cm}}
		\toprule
		\textbf{Widget} & \textbf{Content} \\
		\midrule
		Orientation & roll/pitch (accelerometer integration), \texttt{<canvas>} artificial horizon, zeroing (\texttt{POST /api/zero}) \\
		Odometry & position/heading/velocities (\topic{/odometry/filtered}), trajectory (1200 pts) \\
		Controller \& command & \topic{/joy} axes/buttons and \topic{/cmd\_vel} $\to$ wheel speed conversion (equation~\ref{eq:diff_drive}) \\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabularx}

The interface continuously polls \texttt{GET /api/imu} (consolidated JSON state,
lock-protected for consistency between the \ros{} thread and the HTTP thread). The
dashboard \textbf{shows neither the \gls{slam} map nor the video stream}, two features
of Deliverable~5 (Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations}).

\section{ESP32-CAM Video Stream}
\label{sec:ihm_camera}

The \texttt{esp32\_camera\_bridge} bridge (\texttt{mjpeg\_image\_publisher}) connects as
an HTTP client to the ESP32-CAM's \gls{mjpeg} stream, extracts each JPEG frame
(\texttt{0xFFD8}/\texttt{0xFFD9} markers), decodes it with OpenCV and republishes it on
\topic{/esp32/camera/image\_raw} via \texttt{cv\_bridge}, with automatic reconnection
(2\,s). This stream is consumable but is not consumed by any dedicated vision node ---
no \texttt{vision\_node} (line/obstacle detection, planned in the specification) was
found in the delivered code.

\section{Implementation and Operating Procedures}
\label{ch:exploitation}

The repository provides several shell scripts (\texttt{scripts/}) that illustrate the
low-level/high-level separation, unlike the T1 test in the specification, which assumes a
single \texttt{esibot\_full.launch.py}.

\begin{table}[H]
	\centering
	\caption{Startup scripts available in \texttt{scripts/}.}
	\label{tab:startup_scripts}
	\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{L{3.8cm} L{4.4cm} L{4.4cm}}
		\toprule
		\textbf{Script} & \textbf{Role} & \textbf{Notes} \\
		\midrule
		\texttt{start\_esibot.sh} & manual launch of the calibrator & minimal development script \\
		\texttt{start\_esibot\_all.sh} & production startup (teleop, \gls{imu}, \gls{ekf}, dashboard) & requires valid calibration; radar commented out in the script \\
		\texttt{start\_esibot\_all\_no\_\allowbreak calib.sh} & variant without verification & starts the ultrasonic radar in addition to teleoperation \\
		\texttt{start\_esibot\_all\_no\_\allowbreak calib\_no\_imu.sh} & bringup without mandatory calibration & \gls{imu}, \gls{ekf} and dashboard enabled via environment variables \\
		\texttt{start\_esibot\_keyboard\_\allowbreak autonomous.sh} & keyboard/autonomy command mode & no joystick; expects an external \topic{/cmd\_vel} publisher \\
		\texttt{read\_pwm\_pins.py}, \texttt{read\_wheel\_\allowbreak encoders.py} & \gls{gpio} diagnostics & direct reading of PWM signals and encoder ticks \\
		\bottomrule

\begin{notebox}[title={No script starts the Kinect/\gls{slam} pipeline}]
	The shell scripts are limited to the "low-level control + telemetry" layer
	(Chapter~\ref{ch:commande_bas_niveau}). The mapping pipeline
	(\texttt{mapping.launch.py}) and Nav2 (\texttt{navigation2.launch.py}) must be
	started \emph{separately}, typically from the operator PC (high-level layer,
	Figure~\ref{fig:global_archi}) --- a direct reflection of the three-layer
	breakdown (Chapter~\ref{ch:archi_globale}).
\end{notebox}

Before any startup via \texttt{start\_esibot\_all.sh}, the script verifies the validity
of a JSON calibration file (\texttt{\textasciitilde/.esibot/pwm\_rpm\_map.json},
generated by \texttt{motor\_calibrator}, Section~\ref{sec:cbn_calibrator}), with positive
coefficients required for the 4 \{left,right\}$\times$\{forward,reverse\} combinations
--- a safeguard that blocks startup in case of missing or invalid calibration.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=BashStyle, caption={Full sequence --- low-level startup, then mapping and navigation.}, label={lst:slam_nav_sequence}]
./scripts/start_esibot_all.sh        # teleop + IMU + EKF + dashboard (RPi4)

ros2 launch esibot_slam mapping.launch.py start_rviz:=true        # mapping
ros2 run nav2_map_server map_saver_cli -f ~/esibot_ws/maps/test_room
ros2 launch esibot_navigation2 navigation2.launch.py \
    map:=$HOME/esibot_ws/maps/test_room.yaml use_rviz:=true      # navigation
\end{lstlisting}

The workspace (\texttt{\textasciitilde/esibot\_ws}) groups the \texttt{esibot\_*}
packages, the \texttt{depth\_cleanup} library, the third-party dependencies
\texttt{kinect\_ros2}, \texttt{esp32\_camera\_bridge} and \texttt{m-explore-ros2}, in
addition to the standard \ros{} Humble packages (\texttt{slam\_toolbox},
\texttt{nav2\_bringup}, \texttt{robot\_localization}, \texttt{depthimage\_to\_laserscan},
\texttt{joy}, \texttt{teleop\_twist\_joy}, \texttt{cv\_bridge}). The
\texttt{START\_EVERYTHING.md} file in \texttt{working\_build} documents the build
sequence, the manual startup commands, the topic checks, and map saving.
